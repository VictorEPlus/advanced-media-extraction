# Workspace acceptance checklist

**Status: not formally signed off.** This checklist has not been walked end to end in one sitting. Automated tests and the hidden-window checks pass, but they cannot play video, move a real mouse or judge how something feels, so the items below are what only a person can confirm. Steps 13 onwards cover the newest features and have had the least hands-on time.

Run `scripts/Verify.ps1` first. It builds, runs unit/media tests, and exercises the UI with synthetic media in an isolated data directory. Screenshots are under `artifacts/smoke-*`; no personal media is used and the clipboard is not changed by automated tests.

For hands-on validation on your hardware:

1. Open a frame-extracted folder. Scroll quickly, resize thumbnails, sort naturally/reverse/date/size, and type a search. No growing thumbnail backlog should block selecting another file.
2. Select a photo: no playback timeline, frame-stepping or audio export controls. Details should show its dimensions/aspect ratio and available image metadata. Select a video: playback, exact frames and video exports return. Select standalone audio: only applicable playback/audio tools remain.
3. Collapse Sources and resize the right inspector. All action buttons should remain 32 pixels high; toolbars wrap rather than overlapping. Try 100%, 125% and 150% Windows scaling.
4. Crop a photo by dragging in either direction, including letterbox borders. Copy/paste into an image editor and check pixel dimensions. Reset, then repeat on a paused video frame and step one frame: the crop should persist. No output file should appear until an explicit export command is used.
5. Create a collection, stage individual files and filtered results from two folders, and open it by clicking its name under Sources. Remove an item and confirm the original remains. Save/load JSON, including a deliberately missing path.
6. Add tags to two files in different folders. Use "Tag from details", confirm selected fields, browse all tagged files, and find related files. Restart and verify persistence. Confirm exports include only intended metadata before sharing.
7. Export a full PNG while a crop is selected: it should remain uncropped, matching the documented distinction. Verify trimming/audio export still work in the Export tab, that the tab badges instead of switching, and that a notification with Open output appears. Restart and check Previous exports.
8. Open a long video: the first frame and metadata should appear before the index finishes, then the timeline enables. Hold Right or period to step quickly: frames should follow without a "Decoding" message, and the message should appear only when you jump far away with the playhead. Jump near the end of a long file: it should take well under a second, not longer than near the start. Reopen the same file: indexing should be instant.
9. Type a search that hides the selected file: it should stay open with a "Hidden by filters" note; clear the search and the filmstrip highlight returns. Drop a folder and then a single file onto the window; the file should be selected in its folder. Check recent folders under Sources and the Inspector toggle.

10. Open a folder with several levels of subfolders. The Library tab should list every subfolder that holds media with sensible counts. Open and close folders, click one and confirm the filmstrip and the file count narrow to it, click a graph bar to go deeper, then Clear filters. Select a file: the centre switches to Preview; click Library to go back.
11. Click Tour the UI and step through it on your screen size and scaling. Every outline should sit on the control it describes and the callout should never cover it. Esc should end the tour and put the panels back as they were.
12. Open folder, Browse folder, favorites export/import and Load JSON should all open the normal Windows dialogs.

13. Select a video with sound. The frames per second should be readable at a glance beside the frame counter and match what you expect for the file. The waveform under the timeline should rise where the video is loud, and its playhead should follow playback. Drag a section on the waveform: the in and out handles on the frame timeline should move with it. Play marked range, then Snip sound to WAV and listen to the file.
14. Select an audio file. The waveform should fill the preview. Drag a section, adjust an edge, click to move the playhead, press Space, press I and O while it plays, double-click to select everything. Play selection should stop at the end of the section, and the snipped WAV should contain exactly that section.
15. The Library and Preview tabs should read as folder tabs at your scaling, with the file name, Favorite and Stage on the same row and nothing clipped when the window is at its minimum width.

16. Open a video and wait a few seconds after indexing. Move the pointer along the frame timeline: a small picture should follow it and match what you get when you click there. Drag the playhead and the in and out handles: the picture should follow those too, also while the video is playing. Alt+Tab away while hovering: the picture must not stay on top of the other program. Try a long video (the picture may be a nearby key frame and says so) and a short one.

17. Tick Preview follows scroll. Spin the mouse wheel over the filmstrip in a folder of a few hundred photos: the strip should glide, the preview should keep up with the thumbnail under the amber marker without blank flashes, and the sharp picture should appear shortly after you stop. Drag the scrollbar: same. Scroll to both ends: the marker should slide to the edge and reach the first and last file. Click a thumbnail: it should glide to the marker without the preview flickering through the files in between. Type a search or rescan while it is on: the preview must not change by itself. Untick it: clicking works as before.

18. Select a video with black bars and click Crop & rotate video, then Auto-fit edges: the outline should sit on the picture. Drag each grabber. Click a grabber and press the arrow keys, with and without Shift; press Tab to go round the edges and Esc to let go. Turn the video left and right: the picture turns, and the arrow keys still move the edge you see. Step through the video with comma and period to check the crop elsewhere. Export whole video and play the result: right size, right way up, sound in step. Close the tools, check the reminder line, then Trim selection to MP4 and confirm the trim is cropped too. Try a phone video that is stored rotated.

19. Hover the preview, the frame timeline and the sound strip in turn and roll the mouse wheel: one frame per notch, ten with Shift, and the hover picture on the timeline should keep up. Select a 9:16 video: the buttons should stand in a column beside the picture and the picture should be taller than with the buttons underneath. Go back and forth between portrait and landscape files and check nothing jumps more than once per file. Check the text under the preview is readable at your scaling.

20. Hover the picture and roll the wheel: it should zoom in on the spot under the pointer, smoothly and without drifting. Drag to move around; double-click to fit. Step frames with comma and period, the timeline wheel and Ctrl+wheel over the picture: the view must stay on the same area. Select another file: fitted again. While zoomed, use Crop (clipboard) and Crop & rotate video and check the marks land where you put them. Press F11: only the picture, timeline and buttons remain; zoom, step, export a frame with E, then F11 or Esc and check the panels come back as they were.

21. Play a video, pause, play, pause several times, also after stepping a few frames while paused, and with Play marked range. There should be no black flash, no restart from the beginning and no jump back to an old picture when pausing; at most a very small shift when the exact still replaces the paused video picture. Try a long video and one with a start offset. Select another file while playing, then come back and play again.
22. Open Details for a photo and a video: all the usual facts should fit without scrolling on a 1080p screen, and the aspect ratio should name the closest everyday shape for odd sizes.

Native playback/GPU behavior, actual clipboard interoperability, huge libraries, filesystem/network latency, and all DPI/monitor combinations still require interactive verification.
